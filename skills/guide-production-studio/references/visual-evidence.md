# Visual Evidence for Practical Guides

Use this reference when a reader must judge images, motion, composition, continuity, layout, interfaces, space, or another visible result.

## Why text is not enough

Words can name a problem. The reader still needs to see the evidence used to recognize it. A visual guide that cannot show the judgment may be an honest reference, but it is not yet complete visual teaching.

## Acceptable evidence

Choose the smallest form that proves the intended comparison:

- Input and output at the same useful size
- Parent image and one-variable revision
- Before and after crop of the affected region
- Two or more frames showing motion or continuity
- Contact sheet with selected and rejected candidates
- Annotated frame or diagram with a clear legend
- Real interface screenshot showing the control or state
- Prompt, settings, output, and recorded selection reason
- Short clip comparison at the same speed and framing

## Required context

For each visual example, state:

- What the reader should inspect
- What stayed fixed
- What changed
- What improved, failed, or remains uncertain
- Whether the result was actually generated, edited, tested, or only planned

Do not invent style labels, causal explanations, or performance results from appearance alone.

## Motion subjects

Use a clip or a short frame sequence when direction, duration, speed, or continuity matters. A written arrow may illustrate a term but cannot prove how the movement feels or cuts.

## Model and platform subjects

Separate:

- Official current behavior
- The user's tested workflow
- A practitioner recommendation
- What the supplied result visibly shows

Keep official links beside current controls, compatibility, limits, or parameter behavior.

## Honest incomplete states

If rights-safe visual evidence is unavailable, classify the section as:

- Reference-only
- Planning-only
- Awaiting evidence

Do not call it a complete visual guide.
