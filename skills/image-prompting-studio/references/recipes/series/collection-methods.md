# Coherent collection methods

A collection can feel like one shoot. Define its shared subject styling, environment, image treatment and physical lighting setup. Keep wardrobe construction and key materials explicit; repeat the right details in independent frame prompts. Different frames can reveal a light source differently without changing the setup.

Build coverage around purpose: an establishing frame, hero, useful detail, action/interaction, environmental relationship or closing frame. Pick only those needed. Distinct images do not require different locations, grades or outfits. Keep a frame list to spot duplicated composition and missing coverage, not to enforce ten shots.

For groups, track each person's identity and wardrobe separately. For products, preserve geometry, label/mark placement, finish and scale across angles. Shot-to-shot changes should be stated rather than hidden in vague synonyms. A text comparison can detect contradictions; it cannot prove visual identity.
