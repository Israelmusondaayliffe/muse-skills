# Material and realism review

Apply only the checks that serve the intended scene and medium. A flat vector illustration, sculptural object, surreal world or polished campaign image does not need ordinary photographic imperfections imposed on it.

## People and interaction

When visible, inspect anatomy, hands, grip, joints, weight distribution, body-to-body or surface contact and garment movement. For a photographic naturalism brief, examine facial/skin/hair detail at the scale shown and whether light integrates it into the scene. Preserve the reference's defining proportions rather than automatically averaging stylized anatomy. Do not infer hidden anatomy or inner personality from appearance.

## Materials and environment

Fabric: drape, compression, seams, folds and transparency appropriate to the material. Metal/glass: reflections, edge behavior and material response. Matte surfaces: highlights should fit the finish. Contact: pressure, occlusion and shadows should support the arrangement unless the world intentionally changes physics.

Scene-specific areas: food/utensil use, water/reflection/wetness, steam, crowd repetition, vehicle proportions, instruments and other handled props, glassware, screens, particles, umbrellas, petals and environmental texture. Preserve requested branding and screen/text content rather than applying the old blanket no-logo/no-text rule.

## Lighting and moment

Compare stated sources with shadow direction/softness, visible highlights and reflections. A single source should not create unexplained competing hard shadows; multiple sources may legitimately do so. A no-shadow graphic treatment can be intentional. Do not introduce a rim light without a coherent source when realism is the goal.

A documentary moment may benefit from purposeful gesture, interruption or contact; staged editorial imagery may intentionally be posed. Review against that creative intent. Neither visible pores nor imperfection alone establishes realism.

## How to report

Name the observed area or prompt clause, explain its consequence, and suggest a focused repair. For example, a mismatched hand grip may need a clearer interaction direction; a changed object silhouette needs stronger reference preservation. Do not claim a generic negative list prevents every artifact or convert this review into a numerical fidelity score.
