# Layout methods

## Inventory and spatial brief

Identify each element by a specific description, its count, relative size and visual weight. Record exact copy separately from instructions. Identify the background, target ratio/canvas and reference roles. A reference can supply layout while another supplies content or style; state which role wins where they differ.

Position terms: LEFT/CENTER/RIGHT; TOP/MIDDLE/BOTTOM; FOREGROUND/MIDGROUND/BACKGROUND; upper-left quadrant; lower-right edge. Relationship terms: adjacent, overlapping, behind, in front of, aligned, offset, separated by a visible gap. Scale terms: dominant/secondary/accent, roughly one third of the frame, small relative to the environment.

Use enough coordinates or fractions to resolve ambiguity, without turning rough prose into a promise of native bounding-box control. Avoid mutually impossible demands such as filling every region while preserving a large empty center.

## Ten composition options

- Rule of thirds: place important elements around third-line intersections.
- Golden ratio/spiral: use a curved visual progression through related forms.
- Symmetry: mirrored or centered balance.
- Asymmetric tension: unequal visual mass balanced across the frame.
- Diagonal flow: guide attention through a diagonal arrangement.
- Z-pattern: establish a left-to-right reading sequence where appropriate for the intended language and audience.
- Radial: organize relationships around a center.
- Negative space: give the subject a large quiet field.
- Layered depth: distinguish foreground, midground and background.
- Frame within frame: use a doorway, window, arch or enclosing shape.

These are alternatives to draw from, not a ten-prompt quota. Keep an explicitly requested layout intact.

## Task treatments

**Still life/product:** choose knolling, a casual vignette or a dominant hero; describe physical contact, spacing, scale and shadow integration.

**Character/reference sheet:** state panel count, reading order, view per panel, identity anchors, lighting and optional labels. A four-view turnaround is one composite when requested as a sheet. Distinct perspectives must not become distinct characters.

**Spritesheet/icon grid:** specify cells, common dimensions, gutters, consistent line weight/palette, background and intended labels. Do not claim generated imagery is an engine-ready atlas without inspecting/exporting its actual geometry.

**Comparison:** match scale/framing when that enables the comparison; label states exactly. The ratio follows the deliverable, not a universal portrait/landscape rule.

**Infographic/diagram:** give the flow, component relationships, label hierarchy and leader-line targets. Preserve source facts and avoid intersections that change meaning.

**Poster/editorial:** separate image area, headline, secondary copy and negative space; make reading order and relative type scale explicit.

**Scene/world:** position occupants, landmarks and important objects; establish depth, occlusion and sight lines without inventing unsupported geography.

## Checks

Read the prompt spatially: can each element occupy its assigned position at its specified scale? Are important labels legible at the requested output size? Are relationships complete and directions consistent? Describe uncertainties plainly. Model output requires actual visual inspection if placement matters.
