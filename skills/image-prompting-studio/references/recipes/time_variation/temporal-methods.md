# Temporal methods

## Find continuity in the source

For a character, retain recognizable facial/design cues, distinctive features, silhouette and relationships that the change should preserve. For an object, retain form, components, markings, material identity and function unless transformation is requested. For a world, retain landmarks, layout, scale relationships, palette and fictional premises. Use artistic judgment; there is no compulsory preservation score.

Separate the source's visible facts from invented details needed for the new state. An obscured rear facade, hidden garment or future aging pattern cannot be recovered exactly from a single image.

## Transformation bank

**Age forward/backward:** translate the requested age into coherent proportions, skin/hair, clothing and context while keeping the subject recognizable. Do not assert bone structure never changes, infer personality from a face, or assign wisdom/naivety merely by age. Keep the distinctive stylization of fictional characters when it defines them.

**Time of day:** light direction and quality, available practical sources, shadows and atmosphere should fit dawn, noon, dusk or night. Activity can change when requested or useful to a new narrative moment; it need not change in a controlled comparison.

**Season:** foliage, ground surfaces, weather, light, clothing and use of a place respond coherently. A request for four seasons receives four seasonal treatments, without six unrelated transformations.

**Weather:** rain, snow, fog, dust, heat or storm change surface response, visibility, reflections, atmosphere and interaction. Preserve landmarks and subject identity. A weather change need not imply years passing.

**Era relocation:** coordinate the specified period's technology, materials, architecture, costume and photographic treatment. Distinguish a historical subject shown through a modern camera from moving that subject into a modern world. Do not silently change both.

**Decay/entropy:** material-specific wear, rust, peeling, fading, collapse or overgrowth should follow the stated duration and environment. Preserve recognizability; avoid arbitrary damage or a uniform aging filter.

**Growth/renewal:** show restoration, construction, blooming, regrowth or fresh finishes at coherent stages. Do not restore an object to a different design unless requested.

**Emotional evolution, mastery, cultural context:** retain these source possibilities when explicitly requested as a narrative or fictional exploration. Translate states into visible actions and context. Do not infer biography, competence or cultural identity from appearance alone.

## Output choices

- Single change: one explicit target state, with the source retained where appropriate.
- Matched pair: equivalent scale and framing so the actual transformation is readable.
- Progression: requested states in order, stable identity/world anchors and purposeful differences.
- Open exploration: varied relevant states, with any optional SAFE/INVENTIVE/BOLD/EXPERIMENTAL labels used as craft aids rather than quotas.

The prompt can follow this structure:

```text
Using [the supplied reference described concretely / the described subject], show [target time or state]. Retain [recognizable character, object or world features]. Change [state-specific material, physical or environmental cues]. [Keep the source framing / develop this specified narrative moment]. Light the scene with [coherent sources and atmosphere]. Preserve [important palette, scale and relationships]. [Requested ratio and output form].
```

For an illustrated object or world, time changes its state, not automatically its medium. Medium conversion is a separate artistic choice.
