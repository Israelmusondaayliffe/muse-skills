# Camera methods

## Reference and shot intent

Describe the visible source: character or object identity, shape, pose/state, wardrobe or components, materials, lighting logic, palette, scene and framing. Include a world's landmarks, depth and spatial relationships when the world is the subject. Do not identify a real person from a reference or turn unclear attributes into confident facts.

For a controlled study, the subject stays in the same state while the camera changes. Preserve labels, logos and product design where supplied. A natural shoot may develop gesture, gaze, expression and action. A new shot can keep a coherent lighting approach or deliberately relight when the brief calls for it. State the difference briefly if it affects how the prompts will be used.

## Shot-distance ladder

Extreme close-up: one feature, material detail, eye, hand or object fragment. Close-up: face or similarly tight subject detail. Medium close-up: head and shoulders. Medium: roughly waist up for a person. Medium full: roughly mid-thigh. Full: the whole person/object. Wide: subject plus substantial surroundings. Extreme wide: the subject is small against the environment. Establishing: reveal spatial context, often wide but chosen for what it explains.

Framing terms are descriptive, not rigid dimensions for every subject. A portrait's distance ladder adapts to a machine, sculpture, room or fictional world.

## Perspective and coverage bank

Front; three-quarter left/right; strict profile; three-quarter rear; back; over-the-shoulder; low angle; high angle; bird's-eye overhead; worm's-eye low viewpoint; restrained Dutch tilt; elevated aerial-wide; through-glass or through-doorway; reflection; POV showing what the subject sees.

Describe what the view reveals: profile structure, scale, an object's control surface, previously occluded surroundings, interaction, texture or a relationship. Do not infer an inner personality merely from a camera angle. POV may show the subject's hands or environment instead of their face, which is valid when requested.

## Physical lighting continuity

A camera that moves around a stationary subject sees different illuminated surfaces. Keep the source's world position and quality for controlled views, adapting visible highlights/shadows to geometry. Do not promise pixel-identical lighting. For a new shoot moment, change the light only as the intended scene supports it.

When a template contains sunset, rim light, flash or a changed gaze, use those as an intentional shoot recipe. They cannot override a reference-preservation request. A fixed 16:9 example becomes the requested ratio in a finished prompt.

## Turnarounds and contact sheets

A front, three-quarter, profile and back sheet is one composite image. State panel positions, stable identity/design, background and light. Preserve consistent orientation labels. If the user wants four separate files, say so explicitly rather than returning a sheet. Inspect panel identities and actual views when output is available.

A turntable of an object keeps geometry, markings, material and scale consistent. The hidden side must be inferred conservatively or specified from another reference; do not present it as an exact reconstruction.

## Still endpoints and movement

| Requested movement | Useful still equivalent |
|---|---|
| Dolly-in/push-in | Tighter framing at the intended endpoint |
| Dolly-out/pull-back | Wider framing revealing more context |
| Orbit/360 around subject | Discrete views or a requested turnaround sheet |
| Crane | Endpoint high, overhead or elevated-wide view |
| Tracking | One frame implying movement, such as mid-stride with a suitable blur treatment |

A single still cannot demonstrate a continuous camera move. If the actual motion is requested, identify it as a video task; provide a useful still endpoint only when it serves that request. No automatic dependency on a video plugin is required.

## Panoramic candidate

The preserved 360 equirectangular cabin prompt is a candidate recipe for a 2:1 seamless panorama, not verified support for every model. Use it only for panoramic requests. Inspect seam continuity, projection and poles in a suitable viewer before describing an output as a working immersive panorama.
