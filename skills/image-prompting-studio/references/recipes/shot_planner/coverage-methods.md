# Coverage methods

Use the bank that fits the brief. These are purpose-led options; fixed quotas from the source have become optional presets.

## People: ten-shot editorial preset

| Slot | Framing | What it contributes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Extreme close-up or close-up | Facial detail, expression or a meaningful physical detail |
| 2 | Medium close-up | Head-and-shoulders presence and expression |
| 3 | Medium, waist up | Upper wardrobe and body language |
| 4 | Medium variation | Another action, orientation or relationship |
| 5 | Medium full, mid-thigh | Silhouette and garment proportion |
| 6 | Full body | Complete posture and outfit |
| 7 | Wide or extreme wide | Subject in a setting; environmental scale |
| 8 | Profile or side | Shape and depth from another direction |
| 9 | Rear or over-the-shoulder | What the subject faces and its relation to them |
| 10 | Candid moment | A gesture, movement, pause, laugh or reaction |

For a shorter set, keep shots with distinct purposes. For beauty-only close-ups, vary crop, expression, detail or light only as the brief permits. For couples, give interaction enough coverage to tell the intended relationship; the source's three interacting frames in ten is a useful optional starting point. For groups, consider formation/hierarchy, an individual within the group, and an interaction between named members. Do not assume romance, status or personality from appearance.

## Products: ten-shot commercial preset

Hero still life; extreme material macro; three-quarter beauty; product in use; overhead/flat lay; scale beside a known object; architectural integration; partial detail vignette; dynamic interaction such as liquid or particles; conceptual/mood image.

Keep design, proportions, markings, materials and functional features recognizable. A scale reference must not imply an unsupported measurement. Do not add props or liquid merely because the bank contains them.

## Environments and worlds

Establish the whole space, reveal an important landmark, show depth through foreground/midground/background, examine a material or lived-in detail, use a threshold/window/doorway, and show a reverse view or occupant-scale view when the reference supports it. Preserve geography and the world's rules. Unseen architecture is a plausible extension, not recovered evidence.

## Angle and moment banks

Angles: direct front; three-quarter left/right; profile left/right; rear; over-the-shoulder; low; high; restrained Dutch tilt; overhead; a wide elevated view.

Moments: still/contemplative; walking/moving; turning/looking back; touching/leaning/sitting; interaction with another subject; laughing/reacting; direct camera engagement; looking at a specified off-frame target; a hand/arm/head gesture; arriving/departing. These support a natural shoot. Keep the same moment throughout if the user wants a camera-only study.

## Continuity and coverage

For one shoot, record subject identity, styling, location family, physical light sources, color grade and material treatment where relevant. The camera can reveal the same lighting differently. Time/wardrobe/location may change in an intentional narrative or exploration.

Review useful differences between frames, requested coverage, continuity and physical feasibility. Repeated framing or pose can be deliberate. Note a missing view when it would affect the requested result; do not impose the old ten-frame checklist on a smaller or restricted assignment.
