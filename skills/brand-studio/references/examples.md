# Example tasks

Reusable prompts adapted from real brand work patterns. They demonstrate expected use, not claims that this skill produced any original work. Provide the stated inputs before running each example.

## 1. Turn a campaign request into a brand brief

Inputs: the selected brand, approved brand references and the campaign request.

> Turn this fashion-editorial request into a practical brand brief. It needs vertical campaign images with one consistent model, varied angles and emotions, and a different approved product in each frame. Separate supplied brand facts from your proposed creative choices. Preserve the selected brand and mark missing evidence. Deliver the brief, not image prompts.

Expected result: a campaign brief with audience, purpose, brand constraints, visual direction and open questions.

## 2. Develop visual rules for a second brand

Inputs: the second brand's references and the existing campaign constraints.

> Propose visual rules for this second brand. Keep the campaign to close-ups, profiles and mid-body framing. Explain the palette, materials, lighting and composition supported by the references. Identify what should change from the first campaign and what should remain consistent. Do not invent official brand rules or generate images.

Expected result: usable visual guidelines that distinguish source-backed rules from proposals.

## 3. Review campaign concepts for consistency

Inputs: the approved brief and the written campaign concepts.

> Review these campaign concepts against the approved brief. Check brand fit, product treatment, framing, palette and consistency across the series. Quote the relevant requirement for each problem and suggest the smallest repair. Flag unsupported assumptions. Review the concepts only; do not generate or publish anything.

Expected result: a prioritised review linked to the brief, with concrete repairs and remaining unknowns.
