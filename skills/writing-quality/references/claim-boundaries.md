# Claim Boundaries

Protect the factual boundary of any draft. Keep evidence status visible while ordinary prose work proceeds.

## Classify material claims

Ignore pure opinion, obvious framing, and harmless stylistic language. Classify what remains:

- **supported:** supplied or verified evidence directly supports the claim at its stated scope.
- **unsupported:** no evidence in the allowed set supports it.
- **uncertain:** evidence is incomplete, conflicting, stale, or indirect.
- **non-factual:** opinion, preference, instruction, or clearly marked projection.

## Remedies

For unsupported or uncertain claims, choose one: **remove, qualify, verify, or source**. Do not widen a source beyond what it establishes.

- No evidence supplied → label material factual claims unsupported or uncertain rather than guessing. Missing support is not proof of falsehood, but you don't get to keep the claim as fact.
- Partially supported → narrow the claim to the supported scope.
- Current, medical, legal, financial, security, or platform-behavior claims → verify against the source of truth before treating them as supported. You have web search and a live browser for this; use them.
- Verification unavailable → state the boundary and don't present the claim as confirmed-current.

## Operating notes

- Evidence set = what the user supplied + results from your own tools (search, browser, files, device data). Anything else is unsupported.
- A claim audit does not authorize rewriting; report support gaps with suggested remedies unless the user authorized revision.
- Style edits never fabricate evidence. Removing "slop" is not license to add a fact that makes the sentence punchier.
